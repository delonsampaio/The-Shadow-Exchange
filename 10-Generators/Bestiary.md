# Bestiary — 40 One-Session Creatures

**Use:** pick by dread (Score-Catalog §E) and season. Build the prep file from the entry with `10-Generators/Three-Layer-Monster.md` and `00-Canon/Session-Writing-Rules.md`. **Menace Rule:** the creature is scary on screen; the jokes belong to the people (supers, plumbers, wardens, Greta). **Subway lines (Wrong exit, Cosma, etc.) only after the crew's first Subway visit (S2).**

**Source check (2026-11-20, Gemini):** all 40 Wikipedia links are live at the cited titles. For most entries the core folklore matches the page, but the **weaknesses, Anchors, rituals and local details are this table's inventions**, not folklore: do not present them to players as real tradition. Eight entries (#4, 13, 18, 27, 33, 36, 38, 40) are fully supported by the page. The modern-legend "real example" notes are in each entry's source.

**The Anchor Cover Rule (GM ruling 2026-11-21; `Three-Layer-Monster.md`):** every creature below has an Anchor in its *Is:* line. **Default slip** where an entry does not state one: while the Anchor is whole and near, the creature keeps its mundane cover; separated from it, the cover thins over **1d6 hours** (the standard seizure clock) and the true form shows, starting at the edges. When you prep an entry, **write its own slip** (what it looks like, how fast) and where the Anchor is kept. *(Every entry now has its own **Slip** line, drafted by Gemini on 2026-11-21 and checked by Claude; six entries (#9, 11, 13, 22, 32, 35) also carry an adopted **cover Anchor** because their original Anchor does not work for the rule.)*

**Respect rule for living traditions** (13 entries marked): treat the offering or custom as *working*; never make the spirit or the believers the joke; the antagonist is a human failure (a landlord, a developer, a company).

**Content rule (GM, 2026-11-20):** the table is very open: no content warnings. The one hard line: **nothing racist, and nothing that makes fun of a culture or a religion.** **Anything else goes:** gore, graphic violence, cursing, drowning, grief and fear are all fine. The non-gory versions in some entries (#2, #8) are only a default; use the traditional darker version whenever it suits the scene.

**Flags:** ⚠ marks a canon conflict or an adaptation to apply. Apply them before running.

### #1 The Wall of Eyes

**Where/dread:** **The Overhang** · serious · **Suggested:** Season 1, pick 4
- **Three Layers:**
  - **Appears:** Peeling floral wallpaper in a rent-controlled tenement.
  - **Is:** Echo swarm of staring eyes inside torn drywall. **Anchor:** Worn *go* board wedged behind the hallway radiator. **Weakness:** Patching every tear with fresh rice-paste paper in total silence.
  - **Became:** A widowed tenant watched the street for her vanished son for twenty years until the walls kept watching after she died.
- **Scare / comedy:**
  - **Scare:** Dozens of blinking, unblinking pupils tracking you from ripped plaster; staring back induces temporary blindness.
  - **Comedy:** The stingy superintendent insists it's "structural damp" and tries to fine the crew for scratching the hallway paint.
- **Score seed, choice, thread:**
  - **Seed:** Three tenants moved out overnight leaving furniture behind; Greta flags an unregistered Echo.
  - **Choice:** Strip the Anchor to clear the building (erases the widow's vigil; tenants keep cheap rent) *or* leave the board and patch the walls quietly (Apex scanners keep pinging the block).
  - **Thread:** One eye pupil reflects an empty Subway platform (Omen sign).
- **Slip (Anchor Cover Rule; Gemini 2026-11-21, checked):** **Anchor kept:** wedged behind the third-floor hallway radiator · **Slip:** The peeling floral wallpaper splits from ceiling to baseboard with the dry sound of tearing linen, exposing fresh seams packed edge-to-edge with wet, unblinking eyes that swivel toward the street windows in frantic silence. Plaster dust drifts down like ash as the hallway turns cold and smells of stale radiator steam and twenty years of waiting. · **Speed:** minutes · **Menace:** Meeting the widening gaze inflicts sudden temporary blindness, and fresh tears race into neighboring flats faster than the crew can patch them in total silence with rice-paste paper.
