## The Anchor Cover Rule *(GM ruling 2026-11-21 — applies to every hidden entity)*
An Anchor is what keeps an entity **in the world and in its cover**. While the Anchor is whole and within the entity's reach, it holds its **mundane cover**: its human shape, its job, its ordinary life. That is *how it hides in the real world*, which is why every disguised monster owns exactly one everyday object and **keeps it close** (on a nail beside the desk, pinned in the hair, in a coat pocket, on a thimble finger). It need not be worn.
**Separate them and the cover slips**, in a way that fits the entity (decide per monster): **Merton** changes to his true form (the Surge) · **Mabel** loses cohesion and thins toward the wraith · **Emmerich** unravels over **1d6 hours** (the standard seizure clock, `Campaign-Bible-Appendix.md`). A seized Anchor also pings Apex scanners at 200 ft. That is why taking one is a transaction with a person: the crew holds the entity's whole life in their hand, and the human cost comes before the payout.
When you build a creature (`Is:` layer), always write: *what the Anchor is, where it is kept, and how the cover slips without it.*

## AI Generation Directives
* **Moral Complexity:** Never generate a "pure evil" monster. Every entity must have a specific, mundane person or routine they are trying to protect.
* **Sensory Reveal:** When describing Layer 2, focus on Resonance (smell, sound, temperature) rather than visual "magic" effects.
