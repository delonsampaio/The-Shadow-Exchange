# The Shadow Exchange — Dictionary

> 🔒 **GM ONLY — never read a definition aloud or put it on the TV.** It contains secrets (the Understudied, the Subway's nature). Keep it current: Session-Boot §6 step 4a.

Strict definitions of homebrew concepts. These mechanics must never be conflated with generic fantasy tropes.

* **Anchor:** An everyday object tying a supernatural entity to the physical world **and to its human cover**: while it is whole and near, the entity holds its mundane shape and life; separated, the cover slips (see `10-Generators/Three-Layer-Monster.md` → Anchor Cover Rule). It always has a human cost or memory attached to it. Seizing it is a transaction with a person, not just taking loot.
* **Biological:** A physical, harvestable piece of a supernatural entity. Highly valuable but rots quickly. Must be stored in a Cold Storage Locker or it triggers a Spoilage Clock.
* **Crossroads Broker:** An entity that offers exactly what the crew wants but takes something they will miss later. The cost is tracked in the Crossroads Cost Ledger.
* **Double Bookkeeping:** A specialized crew ability to successfully fudge Ministry paperwork, allowing the crew to skim an Anchor, Relic, or Biological off a government bounty without detection.
* **The Ministry:** The bureaucratic, government arm that issues bounties on the supernatural. Dangerous because they believe they are doing the moral, correct thing. Cover-ups are sacred to them.
* **PTOLEMY:** The brass-clasped ledger or entity within the Subway that records transactions. It is perfectly accurate, completely amoral, and always comes due.
* **Relic:** A supernatural item that does not spoil, but leaks "Resonance" if not stored in a Lead-Lined Vault.
* **Resonance:** The sensory, tactile leakage of the supernatural into the mundane world (e.g., cold spots, clocks running slow, static, the smell of ozone). Not a magical aura.
* **The Subway:** Ghostly and addressless: it appears where it chooses, with no fixed entrance and no regard for physics. The city itself has **no subway system** (the water table is too high; only an elevated train runs). It is the network the Twice-Born use. It chooses; it does not grant. It is never whimsical.
* **Twice-Born:** Individuals who have returned from the Subway or changed states. They are not mindless monsters; they wear wedding rings, carry photos of dead children, and exist in a state of quiet tragedy.

* **Ethereal Credits:** The Subway's internal currency. Non-transferable to the surface economy. 1 Ethereal Credit ≈ 1 Coin in purchasing power inside the Subway. Cannot be spent at Ministry-licensed vendors or used to pay Heat penalties. Earned by completing Subway board jobs or trading Relics with The Fence.
* **Siphoned goods:** Items extracted from a Twice-Born entity using forced resonance transfer (a Null-Tech process, primarily Apex Aegis method) rather than willing surrender or natural release. The item is intact but the process harms the entity. The Fence pays half price for Siphoned goods — the mark is visible to anyone who handles the item attentively.

## Campaign terms (added 2026-11-20)
* **Menace Rule:** Monsters stay scary. The comedy lives in the people and the paperwork, never in a real threat on screen. No comic sting while a monster is present (unless the Run Sheet marks a 🟢 Ruh-Roh).
* **Three-Layer rule:** Every monster is *Appears as* (mundane cover) / *Is* (what it actually is, with an Anchor Item and a Holy Weakness) / *Became this because* (a human tragedy or reason). Template: `10-Generators/Three-Layer-Monster.md`.
* **Season / Mid Arc / Mini Arc / Episode:** The campaign is ~80 sessions in three seasons (S1 = 0–28, S2 = 29–54, S3 = 55–80). A **mid arc** is 3–5 sessions with a climax; a **mini arc** is 1–2 sessions dropped in anywhere; an **episode** is one session. Plan: `05-Sessions/Season-1-Plan.md`.
* **wrap it N:** GM command that switches to the Express Ending Kit and ends the main story in N (2–4) sessions. `05-Sessions/Express-Ending-Kit.md`.
* **Omen (8 segments):** The slow warning clock for the Waking; symptoms, not announcements. Pacing is in Season-1-Plan §1.
* **Decay (6 segments):** The Subway's condition, steered by how the crew treats Anchors and Twice-Born. High Decay breaks the cover or lets the Subway walk.
* **The Understudied:** The Mimic wearing Elias Thorne's face; unmasked at S15 through nine tells.
* **The Architect:** The hidden maker of the Subway; identity is the GM's choice (A–D) in `03-NPCs/GM-Private/The-Architect.md`. Never revealed in read-aloud.
* **Heirloom:** A PC's personal object that degrades in four stages across the campaign; each PC gets a final-stage scene at the end.
* **Heat / Wanted:** Heat is the per-score track; at Heat 9 the crew gains +1 Wanted level and Heat clears. See `00-Canon/House-Rules.md`.
* **Ring El:** the city's only transit rail, an elevated line on the outer ring road (8 stations); never on the city map and never connected to Line 0 (`04-Locations/Gazetteer.md`).
* **Venues Bank:** the 60 places the crew may visit for information, downtime, assets, healing, hiding and vices (`04-Locations/Venues-Bank.md`).
* **Living Map:** The cumulative city map edited one pin set per session (`11-Visuals/Living-Map.md`).
* **Contract card / Fortune card:** TV card styles (`contract`, `newspaper`, `note`, `fortune`, `sign`, `recap`) set through `player-screen/scene.js`.
* **Resonance complication:** A strange, non-lethal side effect of a bad roll near the supernatural (`10-Generators/Resonance-Complications.md`).

## AI Lore Directives
* **Trope Avoidance:** Do not use the words "magic", "spell", "mana", or "epic". Supernatural effects must be described via their tactile, sensory impact on the environment (Resonance) rather than flashy visual effects.
* **The Cover-Up:** Exposure of these elements to the mundane public is not a mere setback; it is an existential threat to the crew.
