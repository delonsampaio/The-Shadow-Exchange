# Teaching Ladder, Session 4 onward

Rules from `Mechanics-Teaching-Ladder.md` still apply: **max 4 new lessons a session**; teach each mechanic **at its first appearance**, in one beat, then use it; if the table already "gets" it, skip; if they don't, repeat; **never** add a new one on top. The GM never acts anything out: the SAY lines are read.

## 19. Biological Spoilage *(S4, when the first Biological begins to rot)*
- **D&D bridge:** like a perishable potion with an expiry timer.
- **SAY:** "A Biological is perishable. It has a clock, **three segments**. It ticks **once every downtime**. When it fills, the item is spoiled and worth nothing. A **Cold Storage** locker pauses it. On an Away Job it ticks **twice** per scene."
- **SHOW:** put the 3-segment clock on the TV with the item's name; tick it after the next downtime.
- **CHECK:** "So what do you do with it before it rots?" ⏸ *(Sell, store, use, or trade it; any answer is fine.)*
- **WATCH FOR:** players ignoring the item. Let it spoil once (the lesson is the loss), unless it is a Gland tied to a choice.
- **GM note:** the S1 Gland starts a Biological before S4; Cold Storage hides it until then (`Active-Clocks.md`, `Clocks-Calendar.md` row 1).

## 20. The Ministry Audit *(S4+, first time a consequence makes it visible; GM-secret before then)*
- **D&D bridge:** like an angry guild or tax collector who locks your stronghold when the books don't balance.
- **SAY:** "Greta's got a form for you. This is the **Ministry Audit**: four segments. It ticks when your paperwork is wrong, or when a Ministry trouble finds you. When it fills, a Ministry inspector locks down Curios & Chronicles: the Vault and Cold Storage are shut until you pay a **big bribe**. You can **pay it down** in downtime."
- **SHOW:** Form 44-C (Ongoing Premises Review, three signatures); the 4-segment clock with the filled boxes.
- **CHECK:** "What's the worst thing about the Vault being locked?" ⏸
- **WATCH FOR:** players treating it as a "paperwork joke". It's a real clock; the funny line is Greta's, never the clock.
- **GM note:** before S4 tick it silently on a Ministry entanglement (`Heat-and-Entanglements.md`). **Never** explain it earlier.

## 21. Subway Decay *(the first Subway visit after Decay goes live, S6 or later, when the red line is first inked on the transit map)*
- **D&D bridge:** like a doom track on the map that only moves when things go wrong.
- **SAY:** "The Subway has its own clock, **Decay**: six segments, shown on the map in red. It rises when the cover-up cracks: monsters leaking, exposure, things the crew failed to contain. At **four** the merchants pack up. At **six** the cover-up breaks."
- **SHOW:** the transit map with a finger-long red line (Decay 1 of 6).
- **CHECK:** "What do you think moves it?" ⏸
- **WATCH FOR:** assuming it can be reset. It can be *slowed*, not wiped. **Season 1 keeps Decay ≤ 3**; do not tell them that.
- **GM note:** the S15 truce and S28 overload never tick it in Season 1 (`Clocks-Calendar.md`).

## 22. Advancement *(the first time an XP track fills; the XP tally itself is taught at the close of S1, see `End-of-Session-Ritual.md` §1)*
- **D&D bridge:** it is levelling up, but per action and per attribute rather than one big number.
- **SAY:** "Your **playbook track** (8 XP) is full: clear it and take a **new special ability**. When an **attribute track** fills (**6 XP**), you take a **new action dot** in it. The crew's own track (**8 XP**) gives a **new crew special ability or two crew upgrades** (each PC also adds the crew's Tier plus 2 to their Stash)."
- **SHOW:** the PC sheet's tracks; the new ability list.
- **CHECK:** "Which one do you want?" ⏸
- **WATCH FOR:** players hoarding XP. Advancement should happen when it is earned.
- **GM note:** the **end-of-session tally** (three triggers, 1 XP each or 2 if it happened a lot; Desperate rolls mark the attribute straight away) runs every session from S1.

## 23. Trauma *(the first time a PC takes it)*
- **D&D bridge:** like a lasting injury or flaw, not just lost hit points.
- **SAY:** "Trauma is the scar. When your Stress track fills, you **take a trauma condition** (the list is on your sheet), you're out of the scene, and your Stress resets. A trauma stays: it can be a hook as much as a cost. A fourth trauma retires the character."
- **SHOW:** the Trauma list on the PC sheet; the player picks one that fits tonight.
- **CHECK:** "Does this feel like the PC you wanted?" ⏸
- **WATCH FOR:** players avoiding stress to avoid trauma. It is a story beat; **no jokes** at the moment (Menace Rule).

## 24. Wanted level *(the first time Heat reaches 9)*
- **D&D bridge:** like a bounty on the party's head that only a conviction can lift.
- **SAY:** "At **Heat 9** the crew's **Wanted level** goes up by one and Heat clears. Wanted level is the dice the GM rolls on an Entanglement (more dice, worse result); Heat is the column."
- **SHOW:** the crew sheet (`wanted:`), the Heat card.
- **CHECK:** "What do you do to lower it?" ⏸ *(Only one way lowers Wanted: someone tied to the crew, or a framed enemy, is convicted and incarcerated. That gives Wanted −1 and clears Heat. Lying low, favours and bribes only lower Heat.)*
- **WATCH FOR:** a player assuming Heat *is* the Wanted level (it is not; see `House-Rules.md`).

## When a mechanic arrives early
If a consequence demands a mechanic before its slot (e.g. a Trauma in S2), teach the card at that moment and note it in the Live Ledger. If more than four lessons fall due in one session, teach the extra one at the next session's first use and log it. Harm is taught the first time it is taken (Ladder card 16).

## After S6: no new cards
Season 1 teaches nothing after the cards above (**except cards 23–24 when first triggered**); later seasons only **reinforce**. If a rare rule comes up (a long-term project, a Cohort), explain it in one line at first use and stop.
