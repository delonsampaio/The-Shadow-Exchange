### D. 10 Pieces of Local Slang (`INVENTED`)
1. **"Sump-struck"** — Bone-tired or rattled after a sleepless night listening to a failing basement pump (also used for hunters who've seen too much).
2. **"Riding the High-Iron"** — Taking the Ring El elevated train instead of driving in the rain.
3. **"Wet-wall weather"** — The three-day November coastal drizzle that turns Old City brick black and makes every stair slippery.
4. **"A Meridian price"** — Paying triple for something mundane just because it was served under track lighting (e.g., "eight bucks for toast is a Meridian price").
5. **"Going over the rail"** — Leaving the city permanently (or, darkly among hunters, disappearing onto Harlan's Wall).
6. **"Overhang ears"** — Nosy neighbors who know your business before you close your car door ("Watch it, she's got Overhang ears").
7. **"Beet-day"** — Any dull, bureaucratic Tuesday when everything tastes slightly wrong (caught on outside the Ministry canteen without anyone knowing why).
8. **"Dry cellar"** — Slang for an impossible lie or a deal too good to be true ("Sure, pal, and I've got a dry cellar in Old City" — makes the Subway's dry steps land harder!).
9. **"Second-try van"** — Anything battered, reliable, and deeply embarrassing that still gets you home (the C&C bookmobile is the poster child).
10. **"Bridge-locked"** — Stuck in gridlock on one of the four river/canal bridges during shift change or a sudden harbor fog.

### E. 10 Ordinary-City Details (Making the City Feel Lived-In)
1. **The Sump-Pump Chorus:** Every cellar in the city sits just above the water table and relies on a float-switch sump pump ([Wikipedia: Sump pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sump_pump)). After heavy rain, whole Old City blocks hum in staggered 12-minute pulses; landlords advertise "dual-battery backup sump" before mentioning square footage.
2. **No Basements Deeper Than Eleven Steps:** Hardware stores devote entire aisles to basement waterproofing and hydrostatic paint; digging a backyard pool requires a county variance and usually turns into a pond anyway.
3. **The 5:15 Fog Horn & Bridge Jam:** Cold bay water meeting warm river outflow rolls fog up the harbor around dusk from October to April, backing up East Gate Bridge and North-Bend Bridge for twenty minutes.
4. **Umbrella Graveyards:** Meridian wind tunnels break cheap umbrellas daily; every café entrance on Glasswalk Avenue has a trash bin bristling with inverted black spokes.
5. **Four Distinct Seasons:** Damp bone-chill winters (slush, never deep snow), green-pollen springs, muggy 90-degree summers when the Docks reek of low tide (Season 2 time-skip flavor!), and crisp amber autumns when Fairground neon reflects best on wet asphalt.
6. **The 2 AM Stagehand Shift:** Fairground's buses run fuller at 2:15 AM than at noon; diners switch automatically to "night-crew specials" once the theater marquees click off.
7. **Overhang Grocery Deliveries:** Because Calloway Steps are 114 stairs straight up, delivery drivers charge a "hill surcharge" and everyone owns a folding canvas rolling cart.
8. **Harbor Seagulls With Zero Fear:** Docks gulls steal whole sandwiches out of hands and ignore loud engines — locals notice immediately when gulls abruptly vacate a pier (a classic Biological/Omen tell).
9. **Cash-Only vs. Tap-Only Divide:** The Meridian is aggressively card-and-phone-tap; Old City and the Docks still run half on crumpled twenties and rubber-stamped loyalty cards.
10. **The Ring El Screech Test:** You can tell a newcomer from a native on the elevated train because natives never pause mid-sentence when the cars hit the screaming steel curve at Bluff-Top Transfer.
