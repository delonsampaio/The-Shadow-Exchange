# Gemini Mid-Arc Ideas Prompt (Season 1 structure, Sessions 4–~30)

Paste the block into Gemini (it has local file access; paths are repo-relative to `The-Shadow-Exchange/`). Claude triages the result per `Gemini-Triage-Protocol.md` — **Gemini suggests, Claude decides**; every file:line Gemini cites is `grep -F`-verified, and anything that breaks a GM decision or leaks GM-private info is rejected.

**Context (2026-11-20):** the table plays **weekly for 2–3 hours** and their past campaigns each ran **2–3 years**. `Campaign-Arc.md` plots a 13-session spine and (§6b) says a normal table will run 20–30+, with a side-score pool, fronts and four Season Two seeds. What is missing is the **middle layer**: 3–5-session arcs that package those ingredients into chapters with their own climax, so Season 1 can run ~30 sessions without feeling like filler.

````
ROLE
You are an experienced tabletop-RPG campaign designer who has run long Blades in the Dark campaigns (faction play, crew advancement, long-term projects) and who also writes monster-hunter mystery with comic relief. You are designing the MIDDLE LAYER of a campaign: story arcs of 3–5 sessions that sit between single-session scores and the main spine.

THE TABLE
A GM plus three friends with 20+ years of tabletop experience, new only to Blades in the Dark. They play weekly for 2–3 hours. Their previous campaigns each lasted 2–3 years. An AI co-GM (Claude) helps run sessions. Target: Season 1 of roughly 26–30 sessions (Sessions 4–~28 still to be designed), with further seasons later.

THE SHAPE: THIS IS A TV SHOW (GM decision 2026-11-20). Sessions are episodes; a season is a story with a premiere, a mid-season finale and a season finale; every session already opens with a cold open and a "Previously…" recap. Design to that grammar: the existing Thorne/Understudied unmasking (Campaign-Arc.md Session 12) is a natural MID-SEASON FINALE (a cliffhanger and a twist). There is ONE ENDGAME for the whole series (the Waking: Subway Decay, the Omen and the Ministry/Apex pressure converge, ending in a choice). Seasons are acts of that one story, not separate stories; the Waking is the SERIES finale, and each season finale is a big turn that raises the stakes toward it. Use TV tools freely: a mythology vs monster-of-the-week mix, a spotlight episode for each of the three PCs (each heirloom stage can power one), one bottle episode (a single location), guest stars (new NPCs who may not last), a cold-open tease for the next arc, and a season-to-season time skip that gives room for crew advancement and new faces.

READ (all of it)
- 05-Sessions/Campaign-Arc.md  (especially §2 Session Map, §3 Truth Table, §4 Trigger Rules, §6 Endgame, §6b Beyond Session 13)
- 00-Canon/Campaign-Bible.md and 00-Canon/Campaign-Bible-Appendix.md
- 02-Factions/ (all) and 03-NPCs/ (all, including GM-Private and Twice-Born)
- 09-Threads/Plot-Threads.md and 09-Threads/Foreshadowing.md
- 10-Generators/Urban-Legends.md, Three-Layer-Monster.md, Resonance-Complications.md
- 04-Locations/ (all, including The-Subway/)
- 05-Sessions/Session-00-Prep.md through Session-03-Prep.md  (what is already written and played-ready)
- docs/review/Canon-Change-Log-2026-11-20.md  (very recent decisions — do not contradict them)
- 00-Canon/Comedy-Playbook.md, Tone-and-Style.md, Mechanics-Teaching-Ladder.md

DECIDED — DO NOT ARGUE WITH THESE
1. Tone: overt Supernatural/Scooby comedy ("laugh, then land") with a mysterious Subway and a dark Mimic/Thorne thread. Comedy never during an Anchor choice or the Subway's first visit.
2. Every arc and every Anchor decision ends in a CHOICE with a human cost, not a boss fight.
3. Canon: the city has no subway (water table about a storey down); the Subway is magical, appears, and has no fixed address; the Containment Suite is exactly one floor deep; nobody boards a train before Arc IV (one pass-by allowed); the Omen and Decay clocks tick on fiction, not session counts.
4. The Teaching Ladder limits new mechanics (max 2 per beat, 4 per session); arcs should plan which Blades systems they showcase (faction play, long-term projects, crew upgrades, Tier, Rep, Heat, Entanglements, cohorts) and when.
5. No safety-tool or content-warning pages. The GM never acts anything out; the AI co-GM presents read-aloud text.
6. Do not reveal GM-private information in anything player-facing.
7. THE WORLD IS ALIVE AND IT CHANGES. Canon NPCs can die, be arrested, defect, vanish or be replaced; new NPCs can arrive; factions can rise, split or fall; districts and places can change hands or be destroyed. Deaths and big changes should mostly be CONSEQUENCES of what the players chose (or failed to prevent), never decoration or cruelty for its own sake. The GM keeps final say on anything marked "protected" below.
8. EVERYTHING CONNECTS TO THE ENDGAME (GM decision 2026-11-20). There is a single endgame (Campaign-Arc.md §6). Every season, mid arc and mini arc must connect to it: it ticks a clock (Subway Decay, the Omen, Ministry/Apex pressure), plants or pays off a Truth Table secret, or moves a faction or NPC toward the final choice. Even a comic one-off episode plants at least one seed or shifts one position. The four end-state families are the possible ENDINGS of the final season, not separate sequels. Plan the clocks so the endgame arrives when the campaign's length (a few seasons, 2–3 years) calls for it.
9. THE STORY MAY NEED TO END EARLY (GM decision 2026-11-20). Life happens: the main story might have to finish after about 6 months (~26 sessions), 1 year (~50), 1.5 years (~75), or at any point on short notice. The design must always allow the GM to wrap the main story up in 3–4 sessions, from whatever state the campaign is in, without it feeling rushed or abandoned. Every season finale must also work as a satisfying place to stop.

DO THIS
1. SPINE AUDIT. Where is the current 13-session spine thin, rushed, or missing a beat a weekly table would need? Cite Campaign-Arc.md:line.
2. MINI ARCS AND MID ARCS. Propose BOTH: 4–6 MINI arcs of 1–2 sessions (one self-contained story with a twist and a consequence that carries forward) and 5–7 MID arcs of 3–5 sessions for Season 1. Each mid arc must contain at least one real TWIST (a reveal, reversal, betrayal or death that recolours what the players thought they knew). Use EXISTING canon first (rival firms, Mara Halliday, the Wall of the Missing, heirloom stages, Urban Legends, Kress's squeeze, the Subway community, Harlan's bounty skimming) and invent new material only to fill gaps. For each arc give:
   - Title and session span (e.g. "Sessions 5–8")
   - The question it asks the players
   - The hook, and how it grows out of the spine
   - Drivers (factions and named NPCs from canon)
   - Escalation, one line per session, marking which sessions can be a stand-alone "episode" the players choose
   - The climax: a CHOICE, with its cost
   - What it permanently changes (crew, city, faction standings, Anchor Ledger)
   - ENDGAME LINK: which of the three endgame clocks it moves (and by how much), and which Truth Table secret it advances or deliberately leaves alone
   - One comedy set piece
   - The Blades system it showcases
2b. WORLD TURNS. A table of 8–12 major turning points across Season 1 — moments the status quo changes for good (a faction falls or splits, the Ministry reorganizes, a district changes hands, a safe place stops being safe, a rival becomes an ally, a secret goes public). For each: the session window, what triggers it, what changes, and what the players can still do about it. Aim for a real shift roughly every 3–5 sessions, and keep the city feeling different at Session 20 than at Session 5.
2c. NPC MORTALITY AND CHURN. A table of every canon NPC and faction leader, marking: CAN DIE / CAN LEAVE / PROTECTED (until a stated session or arc) / HINGE ONLY. For each CAN DIE or CAN LEAVE: the earliest session, what could cause it, what hole it opens, and who or what fills it. Then propose 8–12 NEW NPCs (a name, a role, which arc introduces them, and which canon NPC they replace or complicate). Respect the Three-Layer rule for any monster or twice-born. The Mimic/Thorne identity and the Architect stay under the GM's control — flag them PROTECTED.
3. PACING MAP. A table of Sessions 4–30: spine beat / mid-arc beat / episode slot / downtime-heavy session. Note where the table should be allowed to push the order around.
4. EPISODES. 10 new one-session scores beyond the Urban Legends already in the repo, each with the Three-Layer rule (appears as / is / became this because), one comic beat, one Anchor or moral choice, and one thread it plants.
5. SEASONS. Treat this like a showrunner's season plan. (a) For Season 1: the season question, the mid-season finale (confirm or replace Session 12), the season finale, and three episodes that are pure spotlight (one per PC) plus one bottle episode. (b) A SERIES MAP: how many seasons the one endgame needs for a 2–3 year campaign (propose a number), each season's question and finale turn, and a table showing where the three endgame clocks should stand at the end of every season so the Waking lands where you place it. Show how the four end-state families (Campaign-Arc.md §6) remain the possible endings of the FINAL season. (c) Any season-to-season shifts that change the show's flavour (new status quo, new base, new faction alignment) without breaking the through-line. (d) What carries over between seasons (crew, Tier, Anchor Ledger, NPCs who survive) and what resets, including a short time skip and a "Previously on…" recap line that could open each premiere.
5b. ENDING RAMPS (EXPRESS ENDINGS). Design a plan the GM can trigger at any time to finish the main story in 3–4 sessions. Give: (a) a trigger and a GM command (e.g. "wrap it") that tells the co-GM to switch to the express finale; (b) a 3–4 session finale template that works from ANY state — a convergence session (the three clocks cash out), a reckoning session (the Mimic/Thorne and Architect truths), the Waking and the final choice, and an epilogue; (c) a state-to-ending table: how the clocks, standings and the crew's past choices select which of the four end-state families applies; (d) stop-points: for roughly Session 26, 50 and 75, what the season finale there must accomplish so it doubles as a real ending; (e) a compress protocol: which open threads are resolved in the epilogue montage, which NPC fates are narrated, how unfinished clocks are set (the world speeds up), and what the table should always get to say before the credits (one scene per PC); (f) what to prep ahead each season so an express ending is always a few pages of notes away, not a rewrite.
6. HOLES. List contradictions, gaps and dropped threads you noticed anywhere in the repo, with file:line.
7. DO-NOT-CHANGE. A short list of what already works.

DELIVERABLES (in this order): verdict paragraph; spine audit; the mini-arc and mid-arc cards; the world-turns table; the NPC mortality and churn table (with the new NPCs); the pacing map; the episode list; the season plan and series map; the express-ending kit (item 5b); the holes list; the do-not-change list.

RULES
- Do NOT edit any files. Report only.
- Any claim about the repo needs file:line. Mark every invented idea as a PROPOSAL.
- Prefer ideas that reuse canon NPCs and factions over new ones.
- Do not propose safety tools, GM acting or voices, or content-warning pages.
- Keep it playable by a GM running weekly, reading live, with an AI co-GM; no idea may need more prep than a Session 0–3 style prep file.
````
