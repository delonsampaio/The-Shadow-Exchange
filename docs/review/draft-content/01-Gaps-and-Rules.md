# Gaps and Campaign Rules

The prep for Sessions 0–3 is unusually complete: the writing rules, the boot manual, the Teaching Ladder, the comedy playbook and the season plan are all in place. What is missing is the **weekly machinery**: the routines that run between sessions and the plans for when a week goes wrong. Nearly all of it is short, and none of it touches the story.

## (b) Campaign rules that make sessions go better

1. **The ten-minute envelope.** The contract card is on the TV within ten minutes of the start. Banter and downtime come later.
2. **Two-minute decision clock.** When the table is planning, Claude offers a three-option menu at minute five. At minute eight the GM reads: SAY (calm, practical): "Two minutes, then we go with whatever's on top." This is Blades' own "skip the planning", made friendly.
3. **Every PC rolls in the key scene.** Before the climax beat, Claude checks the spotlight tracker and points the next complication at whoever has rolled least.
4. **One big reveal per session.** If a prep stacks two reveals, the second becomes next week's cold open.
5. **The Menace check.** Before any 🎭 beat, Claude asks itself: is a real threat on screen right now? If so, the joke waits until the threat has left the room. A comic sting never plays while a monster is present unless the Run Sheet marks it as a 🟢 Ruh-Roh.
6. **Monsters get no punchlines.** A monster may have a mundane problem (that's the Three-Layer rule), but the funny line in the scene always belongs to a person: the cover story, Greta, Drennick and Foss, the cat.
7. **Surprising choice protocol (for Claude).** When the table does something the prep didn't plan for: (1) say yes to the fiction; (2) ask one clarifying ❓ if the intent is unclear; (3) look for the nearest prep beat the choice can lead into, using the zig table in `03-Improv-Kit.md` §5; (4) improvise at most one beat, then hand back to prep; (5) log the deviation on the dashboard. Claude never blocks a choice to protect the plan. It protects **canon** (no train boarding before Arc IV, no digging below one storey) and lets the story bend.
8. **Protected NPCs bend, they don't break.** If the table goes after a PROTECTED NPC, the fiction stops them (the NPC isn't there, is warned, or survives at a cost), and that is always narrated as a consequence, never as a refusal.
9. **"What do you want more of?"** is asked after every mid-arc climax, *and* in the three-minute session close (see #12 in the gaps). The answer chooses the next episode slot.
10. **Hard stop at the agreed time.** If a score runs late, Claude offers "cliffhanger here, or a five-minute fast-forward?" Never run more than 15 minutes over without the table saying yes.
11. **Downtime by text.** In short sessions, each player gives their two activities as one sentence each. The dice are still rolled, but the scenes happen only if a 1–3 or a 6 makes one interesting.
12. **Rules lookups take 60 seconds.** If a rules question can't be settled in a minute, the GM rules for the player, Claude logs the question, and it is checked against the SRD after the session.
13. **Cover-up first.** When a consequence is needed and nobody wants harm, use exposure (a witness, a phone, a form) before injury. This is the campaign's signature, so keep using it.
14. **One table-invented detail per session goes into canon,** logged straight away. The players should see their jokes come back.
15. **No silent table.** Every beat names its music. If the music stops, Claude fires the location loop before the next read-aloud.

## (c) Top 10 gaps to fix first (ranked)

1. **One-page House Rules** (#5). It settles the Heat/Wanted question before Session 4 and gives the players one place to look.
2. **End-of-Session Ritual: XP + retrospective** (#6, #12). XP is taught at S4, and the feedback loop steers 77 sessions.
3. **Heat and Entanglements aid** (#9). From S4 onward there is no per-session table; this removes a weekly writing chore.
4. **Faction-Turn Procedure** (#7). "Fronts, not scripts" is the season plan's safety net, but it has no procedure, and the Apex clock is still TBD.
5. **Evergreen episodes / no-prep night** (#2). With a weekly table, a missed prep week is a certainty, not a risk.
6. **Short-session and absent-player protocol** (#3, #4). Over 80 sessions these come up dozens of times.
7. **Downtime Menu with long-term projects** (#8). The players are new to Blades and will ask "what can I do?" every downtime.
8. **NPC Index** (#15). The cast passes 60 this season, so clashes and resurrections become likely.
9. **Derailment Playbook** (#10). Twenty-year veterans will zig, and the AI co-GM needs a script for it.
10. **PC death / player-leaves plan** (#13, #14). Rare, but the endgame depends on every PC's Heirloom, so decide it before it happens.
